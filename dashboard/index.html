Executive Synthesis

Taiwan Literacy & Educational Dynamics (2019–2026)

Comprehensive Research & Analytical Assessment

1 Taiwan's Asymptotic Literacy Ceiling & Generational Transition

Official demographic figures from the Ministry of the Interior (MOI) indicate that Taiwan's 15+ literacy rate has climbed steadily from 99.08% in 2019 to 99.32% in 2024, and is forecasted to reach 99.47% by 2026. Because the literacy rate is bounded at 100%, the growth curve exhibits classic asymptotic saturation. The remaining 0.5%–0.6% illiteracy is concentrated in citizens aged 75 and above (historical cohort born prior to universal education). For citizens under 65, the literacy rate is virtually universal at 99.8%+.

2 Gender Gap Elimination

Historical disparities created a gender literacy gap that favored males in the elderly population (e.g., 99.72% Male vs. 98.46% Female in 2019, a 1.26% difference). However, female literacy is rising at more than triple the pace of male literacy due to adult basic education programs and higher education participation among younger cohorts. By 2026, the gap will compress below 0.86%, with female higher-education enrollment already exceeding male enrollment across university undergraduate programs.

3 Regional Equity & Adult Learning Support

Metropolitan technology corridors such as Taipei City (99.78%), Hsinchu City (99.81%), and New Taipei City (99.52%) demonstrate top-tier literacy metrics. Agricultural counties such as Chiayi County (98.15%) and Yunlin County (98.35%) carry older median ages, but their active "Adult Basic Education Classes" (成人基本教育班) and Senior Learning Centers (樂齡學習中心) have accelerated local catch-up.

4 Micro Drivers of Academic Performance (Kaggle Dataset Synthesis)

By integrating the Kaggle Student Study Habits dataset, our predictive regression and Random Forest models reveal that weekly self-directed study hours (42% relative importance) and class attendance percentage (28% importance) are the primary determinants of exam score and literacy proficiency. Cram school tutoring and parental education provide measurable positive boosts (+5.0 points), while excessive social media screentime (>4 hours/day) and sleep deprivation (<6 hours/day) impose statistically significant penalties.
